Methodology: how the rates are built

Every rate in the Nigeria Construction Rate Book is composed from its resources, never guessed: material quantity x (1 + waste) x material price, plus labour hours x the all-in hourly rate, plus plant days x the all-in plant rate. Overheads, contingency, profit and VAT are added once, on top.

The rate formula

Unit rate = Σ material quantity × (1 + waste %) × price + Σ labour hours × all-in hourly rate + Σ plant days × all-in day rate.

  • All-in labour = daily wage × (1 + 31%) for feeding, transport, non-productive time, statutory cover (NSITF) and small tools, divided by an 8-hour day.
  • Salaried staff = monthly salary ÷ 26 × (1 + 25.3%) for pension, NSITF, ITF, HMO and leave.
  • All-in plant = dry hire + fuel (litres per day × current pump price × 1.10 for lubricants) + operator all-in day.
  • Derived prices: rebar per 12 m length and steel sections are priced by weight from the per-tonne steel price.

Tender rates

Tender rate = net rate × (1 + 7.5% overheads) × (1 + 5% contingency) × (1 + 10% profit) × (1 + 7.5% VAT). Withholding tax (2%) is deducted at source and reduces cash, not price.

Locations

Abuja is the base. Materials, labour and plant are adjusted separately for each location. The factors are starting points based on market evidence and judgement.

Confidence grades

  • HIGH (8 materials): several dated sources from the last month agree.
  • MEDIUM (152): dated 2026 evidence adjusted to the price date.
  • LOW (877): desk estimate or derived; confirm with three supplier quotes.

Prices older than 90 days are flagged STALE.

Weekly updates

Every Monday, key prices (cement, iron rods, diesel, petrol, gas, roofing, blocks, sand, granite and the exchange rate) are re-checked against dated sources. A price changes only when a dated source supports it; a move of more than 25% needs two independent sources. Every change is logged on the updates page.
